feat: the pause gives every clock its time back, and opens everywhere

resume() handed the held milliseconds to two timestamps, because at idle two
was the whole live list. That is what kept the pause at idle — not the timing
rule, which says the opposite once you read it properly. Shift EVERY absolute
reading by the same `held` and no interval moves at all: arriveAt minus
swingStartedAt is the difference it always was, so a swing paused through the
windup grades exactly as it would have.

So the list is the feature. The ball's launch and arrival, the swing's start
and its check, the delivery, the release, the throw and its release, the
flash, the caption, the moment card, the break card, the computer's swing, and
the replay's own scaled clock. One left off does not throw. It quietly expires
behind the pause screen, or grades the next press against ten seconds nobody
played, and the box score is written from the verdict it changed.

With resume() honest, pause() has nothing left to protect and drops the phase
gate. ESC stops a live game on the mound at `calling`, mid-delivery, and with
the ball in the air. The title screen and the final screen still refuse: one
has a keyboard and no game behind it, the other has its own buttons and
nothing running.

The complaint that opened this: a man on the mound could not stop his own game
without first throwing a pitch and waiting out the result.
